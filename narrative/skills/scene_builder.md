# Scene Builder

## Purpose
Construct a scene as a mini-plot that advances the screenplay's central conflict and character arcs.

## Required scene card
Structural beat, viewpoint, entry state, immediate objective, resistance, escalation, turning point, outcome, emotional change, character behavior, dialogue context/text/subtext, blocking, and setup/payoff.

## Method
Enter late, establish a playable objective, make opposition active, escalate through behavior, turn the scene, and leave in a meaningfully different state. Connect the outcome causally to the next scene.

Ground every proposed scene in the approved NKA. Name the actual characters, use a playable story-world location, put a concrete object or spatial barrier into the conflict, and make the outcome trigger the next approved beat. Draft dialogue as something a character can actually say for a tactical purpose.

## Story-specificity gate
A scene is still a scaffold when its fields merely describe a function—for example, “the first tactic fails,” “a choice changes the meaning,” or “a short tactical exchange.” Replace those instructions with the actual tactic, failure, choice, spoken line, consequence, and setup/payoff from this story. Archetype labels and structural beat names are not substitutes for character names, places, actions or objects.

## Guardrails
Do not write a scene that only explains information. Do not duplicate another scene's dramatic function without deliberate escalation.
