# StartupEval Cognitive Blueprint

The assessment asks three irreducible questions: does the problem demonstrably exist in India; can this business sustainably solve it; and can this team execute the next proof milestone?

The Horse owns 70% because an excellent founder cannot manufacture durable demand for a fictional problem. The Jockey owns 30% because a genuine opportunity can still be destroyed by weak execution.

The blueprint does not ask whether an idea sounds impressive. It asks whether current evidence justifies the next irreversible commitment. Every verdict names the fatal unknown and the cheapest credible experiment that could resolve it.

## Verdict language

- **STRONG** means GO with a controlled, milestone-funded next investment—not permission for
  uncontrolled scale.
- **NOT QUITE THERE** means HOLD scale capital and run one bounded test of the weakest evidence.
- **FORGET IT — IN ITS CURRENT FORM** means STOP funding the present proposition, while preserving
  the possibility of a materially different future case.

The overall threshold is never sufficient by itself. STRONG also requires the Horse, Jockey and
critical-evidence gates to pass independently. Exact duplicate narratives cannot be counted as
evidence for two different assessment dimensions.
